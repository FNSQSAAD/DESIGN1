01 — First home buyers

Your first one

The hard part is rarely the loan. It is knowing what you can actually spend, what the government will and will not help with, and what a lender will make of your income.

What we will go through

  • How much deposit you genuinely need, and what counts as genuine savings.
  • Lenders mortgage insurance — when it applies, roughly what it costs, and whether a guarantor or a government guarantee scheme avoids it.
  • Victorian stamp duty, the first home buyer exemption and concession thresholds, and the other government charges nobody mentions.
  • Which lenders treat casual, contract, overtime and bonus income the way your payslips actually look.
  • Pre-approval: what it is worth, what it is not, and how long it lasts.

02 — Refinancing

Switching, and when not to

Refinancing is arithmetic, not a decision of principle. We add up the full cost of moving before we recommend it.

The costs people forget

  • Discharge fee charged by your current lender to release the mortgage.
  • Valuation on the new lender’s terms — and a valuation that comes in low can change the whole picture.
  • Application, settlement and registration fees at the new lender.
  • Break costs if you are inside a fixed term.
  • Resetting the clock — a new thirty-year term can lower the repayment while raising the total interest paid.

If the numbers do not clear that bar, we will tell you to stay where you are, and put the reasoning in writing so you can check it yourself next year.

03 — Investment lending

Structure matters more than the headline

How the loans are arranged decides how easily you can buy the next one, how exposed you are if a valuation moves, and how much flexibility you keep.

01

Offset or redraw

Both park money against the loan. Only one keeps the funds legally yours and outside the loan balance, which matters if the property later becomes an investment.

02

Interest only

Lower repayments now, a larger principal later, and a harder serviceability test. Useful in specific circumstances, expensive as a default.

03

Cross-collateralisation

Securing two properties against one lender feels tidy and can trap you. We keep securities separate unless there is a clear reason not to.

04

Rental income

Lenders shade rental income before counting it, and they do not all shade it the same way. That difference alone can change your capacity materially.

05

Portfolio sequencing

The order in which you approach lenders affects what is still possible later. We plan two purchases ahead, not one.

06

What we do not do

We are credit brokers, not accountants or financial advisers. Tax treatment and negative gearing are questions for your accountant, and we will say so.

04 — Self-employed

When your income does not fit a payslip

Sole trader, company or trust; two years of returns, one year, or none yet. The question is never whether it is possible — it is which lender reads your numbers the way you would explain them.

  • Add-backs: depreciation, one-off expenses, superannuation above the minimum, interest on debt being refinanced.
  • Trust distributions and retained profits, and which lenders will count them.
  • One year of returns — a smaller group of lenders, and stricter conditions.
  • Alternative documentation using business bank statements or an accountant’s declaration, and what that costs you in rate and deposit.
  • Australian Taxation Office debt: not automatically fatal, but it must be disclosed and planned around.

05 — Construction & renovation

Building, in stages

A construction loan does not hand you the money. It releases it against your builder’s progress schedule, with a valuation at most stages, and you pay interest only on what has been drawn.

Where files come unstuck

  • A build contract that is not fixed-price, or variations added after approval.
  • An “as if complete” valuation that lands below the contract price.
  • Owner-builder work, which most lenders treat far more cautiously.
  • Progress payments that fall out of step with the lender’s stage definitions.
  • A construction period that runs long and needs an extension.
