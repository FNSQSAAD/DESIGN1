The basics

What is a Business Loan?

A business loan gives your company capital to grow, cover a shortfall, or move on an opportunity. Businesses use this funding to expand, buy stock, hire staff, or manage cash flow through a slow patch.

Not all business loans work the same way. Lenders price and structure them differently depending on your revenue, trading history, and what you can offer as security. Some business owners put up assets, such as property, inventory, or invoices, as collateral. Others choose an unsecured loan to move faster, without pledging anything at all.

Getting this choice right is a strategy decision, not a paperwork exercise. It shapes your rate, your repayment terms, and how much flexibility you keep for what comes next.

Structure

Secured & Unsecured: What’s the Real Difference?

The difference comes down to one question: does the lender need collateral?

A secured loan is backed by an asset you own, such as property, equipment, or inventory. An unsecured loan isn’t backed by anything beyond your business’s ability to repay it.

01

Secured Business Loans

Secured loans typically come with lower interest rates and more flexible terms because the lender carries less risk. In exchange, you’ll provide an asset as security and generally need to provide more documentation along the way.

02

Unsecured Business Loans

Unsecured loans don’t require collateral. Instead, lenders assess your cash flow, trading history, and sometimes a director’s validation, to decide what you qualify for. Because there’s no asset backing the loan, lenders treat it as higher risk and price it with a higher interest rate to match.

Even so, many small business owners choose unsecured finance. When you need funds fast and don’t want to tie up property or equipment, the speed can be worth the trade-off.

The right call depends on your business, your timeline, and what you’re trying to achieve, which is exactly what we work through with you before recommending anything.

How Commercial Loans Differ From Home Loans

Commercial lending doesn’t follow the same rulebook as a residential home loan. Terms aren’t fixed, and much of it is open to negotiation. A few features worth knowing:

  • Flexible repayments: interest-only options, and in many cases the ability to make extra repayments without penalty.
  • Structures built around your business: choose from a range of security options, loan terms and repayment schedules, matched to your situation and subject to the lender’s assessment.
  • Larger loan amounts: enough capital to buy the property outright or fund a serious expansion, not just a small working-capital facility.
